# BRIEF — Peter van der Spuy

Gathered through direct conversation rather than a single formal interview
pass — the user (building this on Peter's behalf) fed direction iteratively
across several turns, plus independent research (Firecrawl + public Instagram
grids) and a real hero photo the user supplied. Below is that material
organized against the skill's eight topics. Where I've synthesized/proposed
rather than been told directly (journey beats, feeling curve, peak, signature
move), it's labeled as a proposal for confirmation, not a locked decision.

## 1. Vibe

**User's words:** "This website has to be something that makes him look like
a hero... a big thing to explain who Peter is... a hero piece to make Peter
look amazing." Combined with the earlier-confirmed aesthetic range
(premium-minimal) and brand voice rule (specificity over hype, real facts not
filler adjectives).

**Synthesized vibe (proposed):** *cinematic, earned, understated confidence* —
heroic through scale and real specificity (real dates, real places, real
numbers), not through loud graphics or hype copy. Think documentary-trailer
restraint applied to an extraordinary real life, closer to a film's cold-open
than a marketing page.

## 2. Scroll journey

No section-by-section script was dictated directly; this is synthesized from
everything gathered (Porsche Newsroom feature, brand guide, Instagram
research) and the user's "explain who Peter is, give people reasons to come to
this podcast" framing. **Proposed, for confirmation:**

```
1  Arrival        The hero: a Gulfstream on short final directly over Peter
                   on his chopper. Pure "who IS this" hook, no explanation yet.
2  Command         Aviation heritage — decades as an intercontinental Gulfstream
                   captain, the Mandela story (1993, the ANC leadership photo,
                   "Madiba Magic"), heads of state flown.
3  Precision        Motorsport — built his first Porsche from a dismantled wreck
                   before he ever flew commercially, GT-series and hill-climb
                   racing, the 964 cabriolet's six Concours trophies.
4  Wild Africa      The Camel Safari Porsche Project — the rally 911 in Camel
                   Trophy-inspired trim, real named waypoints (Kana Guest Farm,
                   "The Hell Pass"), the cheetah, conservation. This is the
                   proposed PEAK — see §4.
5  The Circle       The podcast as where all of it converges — elite performers
                   like Nick Adcock (350+ races, Le Mans 24, SAES Racing) in
                   conversation with Peter as a credentialed peer, not a
                   neutral interviewer.
6  Follow the      Podcast episode gallery (YouTube-sourced) + social follow.
   Journey         The close: not "the end," an ongoing invitation.
```

## 3. Energy curve

Builds from a quiet, held-breath opening (the jet on final approach) through
increasing intensity across aviation → motorsport → the bush, peaks at Wild
Africa (act 4 — the most visually raw and singular material), then settles
into the warmer, communal register of the podcast/circle before the close.
Not loud throughout — per the brand voice rule, loudness comes from specificity
(real names, real places, real numbers), not from constant motion or volume.

## 4. Feeling curve + peak

| Act | Feeling | What causes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Arrival | Awe / intrigue | The hero image itself — scale, dust, the jet directly overhead |
| 2 Command | Gravity / reverence | Real history — Mandela, heads of state, 20+ years of trust at altitude |
| 3 Precision | Sharp respect | The engineer's-genes detail — built his own car by hand, six Concours trophies |
| 4 Wild Africa | Raw / visceral (**PEAK**) | Real dust, a real cheetah, a real named pass — the least "brand," most lived-in material on the site |
| 5 The Circle | Warmth / camaraderie | Named peers (Nick Adcock, SAES Racing, Le Mans) — Peter belongs in this room |
| 6 Follow | Momentum | The invitation — episodes to browse, a journey still in progress |

**Peak, as the sentence a visitor would say to a friend:** *"There's this guy
who flew Mandela, builds Porsches by hand, and now drives one through the
African bush chasing cheetahs — and he's got a podcast where he talks to
people just as wild as he is."*

**Tell-someone sentence:** *"It's the site where a Gulfstream captain who once
flew Nelson Mandela now drives a Camel Trophy-style Porsche through the
African bush — and finally tells the story himself."*

## 5. Signature move (seed, to sharpen at Step 2)

**Proposed:** a scroll-driven "flight log" device — a small, persistent element
(styled like a stamped logbook or instrument placard) that marks a real date,
place, or fact as the visitor passes each act: *"CPT · 1993,"* *"Simola
Hillclimb,"* *"Namib route · Cape Rally 2025."* Bespoke, coded in the page
(not a kit parameter), and it directly enforces the brand-voice rule (real
facts, not filler) as a visual device rather than just a copywriting rule.
Not locked — Step 2 will test this against the fingerprint registry and the
other seven grammars honestly.

## 6. Aesthetic range

**Confirmed: premium-minimal** — but per this session's "hero piece" direction,
read as *cinematic* premium-minimal (restrained typography and layout, doing
the heroic work through real photography and scale) rather than sparse
corporate-minimal. The two aren't in tension: Apple's own hero-led pages are
premium-minimal and still feel large.

## 7. World structure

**Confirmed: one continuous world** (worldflight is the frontrunner grammar).
Step 2 will still check it against the fingerprint registry and the other
seven grammars before locking it, per the skill's own rule against defaulting.

**Redirect after the first mock-up (v2):** Peter found the scroll-gated text
(cued paragraphs in pinned acts, kinetic headlines) "faffy, jumpy, unreadable"
and asked for a normal, readable site, while explicitly keeping it
story-heavy. v2 is therefore a filmic hero on an editorial body: text is
static and present on arrival everywhere; only the hero photo and the Safari
photo rail are scroll-driven. The arc in §2, the feeling curve and the peak in
§4 are unchanged; they're now delivered by reading order and photography
rather than by devices.
